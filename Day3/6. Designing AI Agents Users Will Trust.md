As AI systems move from copilots to agents that can take action, the hardest problem is no longer model quality — it is trust. In production environments, users must believe that an AI agent will act correctly, safely, and predictably before they allow it to move beyond recommendations. Yet most teams still rely on offline benchmarks and qualitative demos that break down once agents interact with real users, real data, and real consequences. In this talk, I will share a product and systems-level perspective on how we designed, shipped, and evaluated agentic AI systems in production, focusing on the evaluation mechanisms that actually mattered. Drawing from real-world deployments of AI agents operating over large-scale telemetry and decision systems, I’ll walk through how we moved beyond static model evals toward continuous, user-centered evaluation loops. We’ll cover: Why traditional offline evals fail for agentic systems and where they still help How to design online and human-in-the-loop evaluations that measure action quality, confidence, and failure modes The guardrails that enabled agents to act safely, including confidence thresholds, reversibility, and scoped autonomy Feedback loops that turned user corrections, overrides, and hesitation into signals for improving trust and performance How these evals directly informed product decisions such as rollout strategy, default behaviors, and autonomy levels I’ll also share surprising customer reactions — from initial skepticism to long-term reliance — and how evaluation data helped us understand when trust was earned, when it was broken, and how to recover it. Attendees will leave with a practical framework for evaluating agentic AI in the wild, including metrics, system design patterns, and decision checkpoints they can apply to their own products — whether they are building internal tools or user-facing AI systems at scale.
