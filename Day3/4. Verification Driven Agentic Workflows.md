We are in the midst of a fundamental change in how work gets done. Workflow automation has transformed—and continues to transform—software engineering, and is increasingly being applied to broader and broader fields. At this moment, sentiment around the quality of results produced by automated, agentic work is incredibly varied. Within software engineering, we’re seeing a rise in codebases that “work” but are increasingly brittle and difficult to validate: generated faster than they can be understood, over-abstracted, and hard to debug when things go wrong. This talk will introduce a framing and an approach to designing workflows that bring rigor and best practices from the relevant field into the workflows being automated. The examples examined will focus on software engineering, but the ideas are conceptually applicable to any field of work where an open-ended job to be done can be automated by a workflow—a range that is increasing rapidly. As a teaser, we’ll draw on recent research and practices in the ML space that support the models proposed in this talk.
