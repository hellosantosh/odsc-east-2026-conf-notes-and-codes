Enterprise AI agents today operate largely within closed environments—optimized for internal workflows, constrained by organizational boundaries, and limited in their ability to discover or collaborate beyond predefined integrations. This “intranet phase” of agent deployment mirrors the early days of computing, before the emergence of open networks transformed isolated systems into a global web. This talk explores the transition to the “internet phase” of AI agents, where agents act as autonomous economic actors—discovering, verifying, and transacting with other agents across organizations. In this emerging paradigm of agentic commerce, value is no longer fixed or pre-packaged; it is negotiated dynamically, based on context, capability, and contribution. Agents trade not just services, but knowledge, inference, and strategic intent—giving rise to a new market infrastructure for intelligence. We introduce Project NANDA (Networked Agent Nexus for Decentralized AI) as a foundational platform for this shift. NANDA proposes a minimal, open layer for agent discovery, identity, and trust—an index across agent registries that enables interoperability without constraining how agents are built or where they reside. By enabling cross-registry resolution, verifiable identity, and capability-based interaction, NANDA supports the emergence of open agent ecosystems that avoid the fragmentation of proprietary “agent stores.” Through examples spanning healthcare, finance, and enterprise automation, the talk outlines the key primitives required for agentic commerce: global discovery, verifiable identity (KYA—Know Your Agent), privacy-preserving computation, and micropayments for intelligence contributions. We argue that just as the internet transformed information exchange, the internet of agents will transform how intelligence itself is priced, exchanged, and composed—shifting from isolated AI systems to dynamic, market-driven networks of reasoning.
