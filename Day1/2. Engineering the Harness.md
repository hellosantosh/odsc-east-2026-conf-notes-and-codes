Many Generative AI systems struggle in production not because of model quality, but because the surrounding harness is poorly engineered. This workshop treats context engineering as a hands-on systems discipline focused on building reliable, controllable, and evaluable applications. The workshop begins with retrieval, since every downstream decision depends on the initial context window. Participants will work through dense, lexical, and hybrid retrieval designs, learning why lexical methods remain critical for precision, constraints, and exact matching. We will also examine the tradeoffs of introducing reasoning models into agentic retrieval loops. Next, the workshop focuses on memory as an architectural choice. We break down short-term state, long-term memory, and derived artifacts, comparing file-based, database-backed, and structured state approaches. Through concrete examples, participants will see how poor memory design leads to hallucinations, drift, and rising costs, and how selective persistence improves system stability. Finally, the workshop explores single-agent versus multi-agent architectures. Participants will analyze supervisor and routing patterns, learning when parallelization improves throughput and when it introduces coordination overhead and new failure modes. By the end, attendees will have a practical playbook for engineering the harness behind production-grade agentic systems.
